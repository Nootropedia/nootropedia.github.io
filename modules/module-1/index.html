    Module 1 – Foundations¶

    Educational content only

    This course describes research evidence. It is not medical advice, and study doses are reported only so the evidence can be judged. Several substances are prescription-only, controlled or have never been evaluated for human safety.

    1.1 Definition and History of the Nootropic Concept¶

    The term nootropic was coined by the Romanian-born neurophysiologist Corneliu Giurgea in a French-language paper in 1972 (Actualités Pharmacologiques 25:115–156), while working at the Belgian company UCB, where piracetam had been synthesized in 1964 and was marketed from 1971. The concept was built around piracetam's pharmacology, so its originator was also employed by the company selling the first nootropic. In animal models piracetam protected learned behavior against hypoxia and electroconvulsive shock without clear sedative or GABAergic effects, and Giurgea proposed it as a new pharmacological class.

    According to Giurgea, a nootropic should:

    1. Enhance learning and memory.
    2. Increase the resistance of learned behavior to disruptive conditions (hypoxia, electroconvulsive shock, scopolamine).
    3. Protect the brain against physical or chemical injury.
    4. Increase the efficacy of tonic cortical and subcortical control mechanisms.
    5. Lack the usual pharmacology of psychotropic drugs (no sedation, no motor stimulation), with very few side effects and extremely low toxicity.

    These criteria were derived almost entirely from rodent paradigms, whose translation to human cognition has proven poor. The fifth criterion also excludes most substances that actually produce measurable effects in humans. [Established] Strictly applied, almost no substance with replicated human efficacy meets all five criteria, and the substances that come closest to criterion five (racetams) have the weakest human efficacy data.

    Psychostimulant enhancers Catecholaminergic or adenosinergic arousal Caffeine, methylphenidate, modafinil
    Cholinergic agents Precursors, esterase inhibitors, receptor modulators Citicoline, Alpha-GPC, donepezil

    1.2 Neurotransmitter Systems Relevant to Cognition¶

    Cholinergic system. Cortical and hippocampal acetylcholine derives mainly from the basal forebrain (nucleus basalis of Meynert, medial septum, diagonal band). [Established] Cholinergic tone is closely linked to attention, encoding, and cue detection. The homomeric α7 nAChR has high Ca²⁺ permeability and modulates glutamate release and plasticity; α4β2 is the high-affinity nicotine site involved in attention. M1 (Gq, cortex and hippocampus) is the muscarinic subtype most tied to memory, shown most clearly by the pro-amnestic effect of scopolamine, the standard challenge model in nootropic research. Under high demand, choline uptake via CHT1 (SLC5A7) is rate-limiting for acetylcholine synthesis. [Hypothesis] Choline donor supplementation assumes choline supply limits synthesis, which is doubtful in well-nourished humans.

    Glutamatergic system. AMPA receptors (GluA1–4) mediate most fast excitatory current; their kinetics are defined by deactivation and desensitization, which positive allosteric modulators slow. [Hypothesis] for the racetams, since relevant in vitro concentrations often exceed plausible brain levels. NMDA receptors are coincidence detectors requiring glutamate, a co-agonist, and depolarization to relieve the Mg²⁺ block. GluN2A-containing receptors dominate synaptically in the adult forebrain; GluN2B-containing receptors are enriched extrasynaptically and linked to both plasticity and excitotoxic signaling. Too little NMDA activity impairs plasticity; too much is excitotoxic.

    Catecholaminergic systems. Dopaminergic VTA projections and noradrenergic locus coeruleus projections are central to working memory and executive control. [Established] Prefrontal function follows an inverted-U dose-response curve: both too little and too much D1 stimulation impair working-memory-related persistent firing. Moderate noradrenaline acts on α2A receptors, which reduce cAMP and close HCN channels on dendritic spines, strengthening network connectivity (Wang et al., 2007, Cell). High noradrenaline during stress recruits α1 and β receptors and weakens PFC function. Mattay et al. (2003, PNAS) showed that amphetamine improved prefrontal efficiency during a working-memory task in COMT val/val carriers (presumably lower prefrontal dopamine), whereas in met/met carriers it had no effect at low-to-moderate load and worsened efficiency at the highest load. The effect of a dose depends on where the individual starts on the curve.

    Adenosinergic system. Adenosine accumulates with neuronal activity and energy turnover and acts as a homeostatic brake on arousal. A1 receptors (Gi/o) inhibit presynaptic release; A2A receptors (Gs/olf) are concentrated in the striatum and form heteromers with D2 receptors. [Established] Caffeine's arousal effects are mediated primarily by antagonism at these receptors.

    GABAergic, serotonergic and histaminergic modulation. GABAergic inhibition shapes gamma and theta oscillations; GABA-A positive modulators such as benzodiazepines are reliably amnestic. 5-HT4 agonism increases hippocampal acetylcholine release. [Established] The failure of 5-HT6 antagonists (idalopirdine, intepirdine) in phase III Alzheimer's trials shows robust rodent procognitive effects failing to translate. H3 inverse agonism (pitolisant) promotes wakefulness and helps explain modafinil's downstream effects.

    1.3 Neuroplasticity and Neurotrophins¶

    Synaptic plasticity. LTP was first described by Bliss and Lømo (1973, Journal of Physiology). In E-LTP, NMDA-mediated Ca²⁺ influx activates CaMKII, which autophosphorylates at Thr286, phosphorylates GluA1 at Ser831, and promotes AMPA receptor insertion; PKA contributes via GluA1 Ser845. L-LTP requires CREB phosphorylation at Ser133, gene transcription (including BDNF), and spine remodeling. LTD involves calcineurin, PP1, and AMPA internalization. [Hypothesis] Plasticity is a regulated signal-to-noise process, and indiscriminate facilitation could strengthen irrelevant associations as readily as relevant ones.

    BDNF. Mature BDNF binds TrkB and activates PI3K/Akt, MAPK/ERK, and PLCγ cascades, required for hippocampal L-LTP. proBDNF binds p75NTR with sortilin and promotes LTD and spine retraction. Egan et al. (2003, Cell) showed that the Val66Met Met allele impairs activity-dependent BDNF secretion and is associated with poorer episodic memory. Later replication has not held up: a meta-analysis of 23 publications with 7,095 people found no significant association between Val66Met and any cognitive phenotype (Mandelman & Grigorenko, 2012, Genes, Brain and Behavior 11:127–136, doi:10.1111/j.1601-183X.2011.00738.x).

    Methodological caution. Serum BDNF is dominated by platelet stores released during clotting and is affected by exercise, time of day, and handling. [Established] A rise in serum BDNF is not evidence of increased central neurotrophic signaling, let alone of cognitive benefit.

    NGF. NGF acts via TrkA and p75NTR and is essential for basal forebrain cholinergic neurons. [Established] It does not cross the BBB, and intracerebroventricular NGF in early Alzheimer's trials caused pain and weight loss. Raising NGF is neither easy to achieve in the right place nor clearly beneficial when achieved non-selectively.

    1.4 Cerebral Energy Metabolism¶

    [Established] The brain accounts for about 2% of body mass but about 20% of resting energy consumption, mostly spent on Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Glucose enters via GLUT1 and GLUT3. The astrocyte–neuron lactate shuttle (Pellerin & Magistretti, 1994, PNAS) proposes that astrocytic glycolysis supplies lactate to neurons via MCTs; [Hypothesis] its quantitative importance in vivo remains debated.

    The phosphocreatine/creatine kinase system buffers ATP at sites of high turnover. The brain synthesizes creatine and also takes it up via SLC6A8 at the blood–brain barrier, but only in limited amounts, which limits the brain increase from supplementation (Braissant, 2012, Journal of Inherited Metabolic Disease 35:655–664, doi:10.1007/s10545-011-9433-2); Dechent et al. (1999) measured a rise of roughly 8–9% (Module 2.3). SLC6A8 loss-of-function causes a severe creatine deficiency syndrome, showing that brain creatine is essential, not that extra creatine helps. [Established] Restoring mitochondrial function in deficit models is different from enhancing normal function in healthy tissue. Ketone bodies matter mainly in fasting and in conditions with impaired glucose use.

    1.5 The Blood–Brain Barrier¶

    The BBB is formed by brain capillary endothelial cells with tight junctions (claudin-5, occludin, ZO proteins), supported by pericytes and astrocytic end-feet.

    Property Typical CNS-favorable range
    Molecular weight < ~450 Da
    TPSA < ~90 Å²
    Hydrogen-bond donors ≤ 3
    logP Moderate, roughly 2–4
    Ionization Minimal charge at physiological pH
    Transporter Substrates Relevance
    LAT1 (SLC7A5) Tyrosine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, leucine Tyrosine competes with other LNAAs
    CTL1, OCTs Choline Limited, saturable uptake
    MFSD2A DHA as LPC-DHA Main DHA route (Nguyen et al., 2014, Nature)
    SLC6A8 Creatine Limits brain creatine increase
    MCT1 Lactate, ketone bodies Energy substrates
    GLUT1 Glucose Primary energy supply

    Efflux transporters (P-gp/ABCB1, BCRP/ABCG2) pump many lipophilic compounds back into blood; P-gp substrate status has never been tested for most nootropics. [Established] Peptides generally have minimal BBB penetration and poor oral bioavailability. [Hypothesis] Nose-to-brain delivery exists, but the fraction reaching the brain is usually very small and, for most peptide nootropics, never quantified in humans. The question "Does it reach the target?" should come before "What does it do at the target?"

    1.6 Methodology of Cognition Research¶

    Domain Typical tests
    Sustained attention/vigilance PVT, CPT, SART
    Working memory n-back, digit span, spatial working memory
    Episodic memory RAVLT, word-list recall, paired-associate learning
    Executive function Stroop, Trail Making B, Wisconsin Card Sorting, stop-signal
    Processing speed Digit Symbol Substitution, reaction time
    Subjective state Visual analogue scales, POMS

    Standardized batteries include CANTAB, the CDR System, and Cogstate; they generate many outcomes per session, which creates a multiple-testing problem.

    Typical weaknesses include practice effects, ceiling effects in healthy young people, uncorrected multiple testing, and low power. Button et al. (2013, Nature Reviews Neuroscience) estimated the median power of neuroscience studies at about 21%, which also inflates significant effect sizes. Improvements may reflect restoration rather than enhancement, as with caffeine withdrawal reversal (James & Rogers, 2005, Psychopharmacology 182:1–8, doi:10.1007/s00213-005-0084-6; Rogers et al., 2013, Module 2.1). Baseline dependency can hide opposing subgroup effects. Substances with noticeable effects are hard to blind, and few trials assess blinding. Most trials are short and rely on surrogate endpoints. Manufacturer funding, publication bias, and hard-to-access, unreplicated Soviet/Russian literature further distort the picture.

    Question Warning sign
    Pre-registered with a primary endpoint? Positive result on a secondary or unspecified measure
    Sample large enough? n < 50 with a claimed medium-to-large effect
    Who was studied? Deficient or clinical population generalized to healthy users
    Blinding assessed? Noticeable subjective effects, blinding untested
    Multiple comparisons corrected? One significant subtest among many
    Who funded it? Manufacturer, without independent replication
    Independent replication? All positive data from one lab or one country
    Plausible given PK? Mechanism based on concentrations never shown in brain
