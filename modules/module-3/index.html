    Module 3 – Moderately Supported Substances¶

    Educational content only

    This course describes research evidence. It is not medical advice, and study doses are reported only so the evidence can be judged. Several substances are prescription-only, controlled or have never been evaluated for human safety.

    These substances have a plausible rationale and some human data, but major gaps: small samples, industry involvement, clinical populations generalized to healthy users, or unclear pharmacokinetics.

    3.1 Rhodiola rosea¶

    Origin & History. A succulent of cold mountainous regions used in Scandinavian, Russian and Siberian folk medicine for fatigue. Nikolai Lazarev coined "adaptogen" in 1947; Soviet researchers studied Rhodiola from the 1960s.

    Evidence rating. C for fatigue under stress; D for rested healthy people. Safety: partially characterized.

    3.2 Citicoline (CDP-Choline)¶

    Origin & History. An endogenous Kennedy-pathway intermediate described by Kennedy and Weiss (1956, Journal of Biological Chemistry). Developed as a drug by Ferrer and approved in several countries for stroke, brain injury and cognitive impairment.

    Evidence rating. C for healthy people; upper C for older adults with memory complaints; stroke evidence negative. Safety: well characterized.

    3.3 Alpha-GPC (Choline Alfoscerate)¶

    Origin & History. A phospholipid breakdown product marketed in Italy as Gliatilin (Italfarmaco) for dementia; widely prescribed in South Korea; a supplement in the US.

    Evidence rating. C for dementia; D for healthy people. Safety: partially characterized, with an unresolved stroke signal.

    3.5 L-Tyrosine¶

    Origin & History. Isolated by Justus von Liebig in 1846 from casein. Interest came from Wurtman's precursor research and US and Dutch military stress research.

    Evidence rating. B−/C under catecholamine-depleting stress; D unchallenged. Safety: partially characterized.

    3.6 Ginkgo biloba (short monograph)¶

    The standardized extract EGb 761 (Dr. Willmar Schwabe) contains 22–27% flavonol glycosides and 5–7% terpene lactones, with ginkgolic acids below 5 ppm.

    Rating: C for dementia; robustly null for prevention and healthy people.
